Research

Neural dynamics beyond linearization

Shared noisy input to a neural population doesn’t average away in the large-N limit, and it reaches the firing rates through a nonlinear transfer function. I decompose the nonlinear functions of the noise into Gaussian processes matched in mean and full temporal covariance. The resulting mean-field theory captures strong-noise transients and a noise-shifted Hopf bifurcation that linearization can’t.

Escape rates beyond Kramers

Kramers’ escape rate assumes weak noise and a quadratic well; near a fold, where a metastable state is about to disappear, neither holds. The dynamical moment hierarchy (DMH) I developed stays accurate at strong noise and in wells far from quadratic, without a moment closure. I’ve extended this into a theory of stochastic bifurcations that accounts for ghost states and for the way noise smudges a bifurcation.

Spatial structure, noise, and chaos

The organization of fluctuations matters as much as their strength. I study how spatially correlated noise suppresses chaos and affects pattern formation in recurrent excitatory–inhibitory networks, combining neural-field models with stochastic simulation and Lyapunov analysis.
